Abstract 
Barangay hearing scheduling has to be more effective due to the growing 
complexity of legal cases in Philippine communities. This thesis presents the 
HearEase system, which uses deep neural networks to optimize the scheduling 
of barangay hearings and predict case resolution times. The study recognizes a 
number of flaws in traditional scheduling techniques that frequently lead to both 
unresolved cases and postponed hearings. HearEase, a pilot project in Barangay 
Tetuan, Zamboanga City, Philippines, was chosen because of the community's 
active involvement, the volume of cases it handles each year, and their 
willingness to fully cooperate with the study. 
By utilizing various advanced algorithms, HearEase aims to provide 
barangay staff with a user-friendly interface which automates scheduling per 
historical data with predictive analytics. The methodology involves gathering data 
from past hearings, case results, and the implementation of deep learning 
models for prediction of future case resolutions. Initial outcomes indicate that the 
implementation of HearEase has the capacity to enhance the effectiveness of 
barangay hearings, ensuring resolutions of cases in a timely manner. In addition 
to reducing the administrative load on the Lupong Tagapamayapa and barangay 
employees, this system seeks to enhance the accessibility of justice for the local 
populace. 
Keywords: Philippine governance, barangay hearings, deep neural network, 
resolution prediction, scheduling optimization

CHAPTER I​
INTRODUCTION 
 
Background of the Study 
With its function as the first level of government, barangay hearings play a 
vital role in resolving conflicts and maintaining community peace in the 
Philippines. Though important, long wait times and scheduling issues often call 
into question the effectiveness of barangay hearings. Especially for people in 
communities who depend on quick and fair choices, these elements disturb the 
tranquility of the community and act as obstacles to justice. Ignoring these 
obstacles could make barangay hearings face challenges in meeting community 
needs and offering prompt case resolutions, therefore hindering social progress. 
Often run traditionally by writing everything down, which is error-prone and 
time-consuming, barangay hearings face one of the biggest obstacles to 
success. Resolving a case presented to the barangay hearing format on an ad 
hoc basis creates more problems and drags out the dispute since the mode of 
justice becomes ineffective. The unwieldy nature of justice diminishes confidence 
in the entire system because residents do not see the outcome of a hearing in a 
timely manner.  
On top of this, there are no new solutions that can improve scheduling, the 
timing of resolutions, and resource allocation. Automated systems would help 
 
12 
reduce human error and inefficiencies in barangay hearings, which can lead to 
delays and unresolved cases. The inability to address this variety of challenging 
situations could be inconvenient for residents and barangay officials, especially 
when they feel overwhelmed by several cases that continue to grow in volume 
with limited resources. The lack of technological solutions means that there will 
not be enough improvements made for the efficiency of hearings and that delays 
and frustrations continue in the community. 
Many studies have looked at how technology can be used in ways to 
increase efficiency in administrative processes in the public sector. For example, 
a study evaluated the effective ways to implement AI-based systems for the 
municipality of Carmona, Cavite. The incorporation of automation increased the 
number of resource allocations, thus minimizing delays due to inefficient manual 
processes [1]. Another study conducted by Balilia et al. looked at how 
implementing automated systems in barangay offices improved administrative 
function related to automating project monitoring and record keeping as well. 
Notable improvements in efficiency increased public service delivery to residents 
[2]. The authors propose a more comprehensive system that focuses on 
administrative functions such as monitoring projects and processing documents, 
while excluding the consideration of barangay hearing procedures from the 
study’s scope. 
Melnik and Nasonov studied how Deep Neural Networks (DNN) and 
Reinforcement Learning can be used for scheduling optimization in general 
 
13 
workflow systems. They did report slightly positive results in which workflow 
assignments were enhanced and reduced processing times [3]. While they 
provide evidence that could demonstrate a positive improvement to several 
workflows developed, it has not been tested in other administrative 
environments, specifically barangay hearings, which involve a high structure of 
procedures, complex logics, and limits in resources. 
Alternatively, a relevant study examined the use of Artificial Intelligence 
(AI) as a supplementary tool in judicial hearings. Their study goes further by 
proposing that AI could also assist in legal practice, particularly in adjudicating 
disputes. They suggested that AI possess the possibility to contribute to legal 
expertise or judgement, and even envisioned scenarios where cases could be 
fully resolved by a robotic judge handling the entire adjudication process. 
However, the issue of fairness was mostly limited to the extreme position of AI 
providing the full resolution outcome. They suggested that working with artificial 
intelligence as a support for decision-making would be fitting to apply human 
expertise [4]. 
Lastly, Bringula et al. examined the creation of e-Barangay, a web-based 
system meant to improve local government at the Philippines' barangay level. 
Their research developed an e-Barangay framework with elements including 
complaint processing, document requests, reminders and alerts. Using both 
subjective and objective criteria, the system's usability was assessed. The results 
revealed that e-Barangay satisfied local needs. The e-Barangay system 
 
14 
efficiently completed tasks in objective measurements and had positive feedback 
in subjective evaluations [5]. 
This study presents the development of HearEase, a web-based platform 
that leverages a Deep Neural Network (DNN) to optimize the scheduling of 
barangay hearings and predict case resolution timelines.  
Designed for web accessibility, the system features distinct, role-based 
portals for barangay officials and a transparent mechanism for citizens to file 
complaints and track case status. By automating the scheduling process 
according to established Katarungang Pambarangay rules, the system aims to 
enhance administrative efficiency, minimize procedural delays, and facilitate the 
delivery of timely justice to the community. 
This system will begin implementation in Barangay Tetuan in Zamboanga 
City for several important reasons. To begin with, Barangay Tetuan is among the 
largest barangays in Zamboanga City in terms of caseload and will therefore 
provide an opportunity to test the impact of the program without being vastly 
limited by its size. Having also a diverse population means that we can expect a 
broad selection of cases that can be used to assess the impact of the program 
across different demographics. Additionally, the local officials in Barangay Tetuan 
expressed that they will also provide a receptive environment for technological 
improvements in administrative processes. Their local officials welcomed the idea 
of incorporating technology to strengthen administrative processes in their 
 
15 
Barangay. Lastly, Barangay Tetuan produces good records of case hearings and 
resolutions. Therefore, available data points to train the AI system in the most 
effective way will be accessible. The study wishes to use Barangay Tetuan as a 
pilot study to collect effective feedback and outputs for disseminating the study to 
other barangays in the same region in the future. 
 
Statement of the Problem 
While the barangay justice system plays a vital role in local dispute 
resolution, Barangay Tetuan continues to rely on traditional, manual scheduling 
processes that are often unstructured and susceptible to human error. This 
reliance on ad-hoc methods frequently results in scheduling conflicts, resource 
mismanagement, and inconsistent estimation of hearing durations.  
 
Consequently, these inefficiencies lead to procedural delays and potential 
inequity in accessing swift justice for complainants. Currently, there is a lack of 
data-driven mechanisms that utilize historical patterns to optimize hearing 
schedules and predict case resolution timelines, leaving the barangay unable to 
cope effectively with its increasing caseload. 
 
 
16 
Objectives 
This study aims to build an artificial intelligence-driven system employing 
Deep Neural Networks (DNN) to maximize barangay hearing schedules and 
forecast case resolution times. 
 
Specifically, the study will: 
●​ Analyze the existing case resolution workflows and scheduling constraints 
in Barangay Tetuan to identify requirements for automation.  
●​ Collect case data to be used to train the neural network for schedule 
prediction. 
●​ Develop HearEase, a web-based information system that integrates 
AI-driven scheduling and case management features.  
●​ Evaluate the predictive accuracy of the DNN model in forecasting hearing 
frequency and resolution timelines.  
●​ Assess the system’s usability, efficiency, and user satisfaction with the 
Lupong Tagapamayapa compared to traditional manual procedures. 
 
Scope and Limitations 
Scope of the Study  
This study focuses on the design, development, and deployment of 
"HearEase," a web-based hearing scheduling and management system 
integrated with a Deep Neural Network (DNN) model. The primary objective is to 
 
17 
optimize the administrative workflow of the Katarungang Pambarangay by 
automating scheduling procedures and predicting case resolution timelines 
based on historical data patterns.  
The study is conducted within the specific context of Barangay Tetuan, 
Zamboanga City. The system is designed to serve two distinct user groups:  
1. Barangay Administrators (Punong Barangay, Barangay Secretary, and 
Lupong Tagapamayapa Secretary) 
The system provides them with a dashboard for digital case filing, 
automated hearing scheduling, notification management, and analytical 
reporting.  
2. Constituents (Residents) 
The system provides a public-facing portal for filing complaints online and 
tracking the real-time status of their cases.  
Technically, the system’s scope covers the full cycle of case 
management—from the filing of the complaint to the issuance of the final 
certification (Settled, Arbitration, or Certificate to File Action). The predictive 
capabilities are limited to estimating the number of hearings and resolution 
duration using a dataset constructed from Barangay Tetuan’s case records. 
 
18 
Limitations of the Study 
To ensure the feasibility and focus of the research, the following limitations 
are established:  
1. Geographical and Contextual Limit 
The system is customized specifically for the operational workflow 
and volume of Barangay Tetuan. While the underlying logic may be 
applicable elsewhere, the dataset used to train the AI is localized; 
therefore, the prediction accuracy may not be immediately generalizable 
to other barangays with different demographic profiles without re-training.  
2. Administrative vs. Judicial Role 
The AI model is strictly limited to administrative scheduling and time 
estimation. It does not attempt to predict the legal outcome of a case nor 
suggest specific settlement terms. The discretionary power to mediate and 
decide on cases remains exclusively with the Lupon Tagapamayapa, in 
accordance with the Local Government Code.  
3. Jurisdictional Limit on Fines 
The system's case management is limited to disputes falling within 
the Katarungang Pambarangay’s jurisdiction, specifically those that do not 
 
19 
involve a fine exceeding Five Thousand Pesos (₱5,000), as per the rules 
of the Local Government Code. 
3. Parties in Conflict 
The system will only process complaints between natural persons 
residing or working in Barangay Tetuan. It will not cater to complaints filed 
by or against organizations, corporations, or non-residents of the 
barangay. This limitation is necessary because the jurisdiction of the 
Katarungang Pambarangay, which HearEase is designed to support, is 
generally confined to disputes between natural persons who are residents 
of the same, contiguous, or specific barangays, and does not extend to 
juridical entities like corporations, pursuant to the Local Government Code 
of 1991. 
4. Procedural Adherence 
While the system allows for online filing, it does not replace the 
legal requirement for face-to-face confrontation. The system serves as a 
logistical tool to facilitate the scheduling of these in-person meetings. The 
issuance of summons and certifications will still be physically printed and 
served by Barangay Tanods as required by law, though the documents are 
generated digitally.  
5. User Adoption 
 
20 
The system is web-based and requires an internet connection to 
function. The study evaluates the usability and efficiency of the system but 
does not measure the long-term behavioral change of the residents 
regarding digital adoption. The effectiveness of the system relies on the 
willingness of the barangay staff to transition from manual logbooks to 
digital encoding. 
Significance of the Study 
The development and implementation of HearEase offers significant 
contributions to the modernization of the barangay justice system. By integrating 
Artificial Intelligence into local governance, this study yields specific benefits for 
the following stakeholders:  
For the Barangay Officials and Lupong Tagapamayapa 
As the primary users, this system directly addresses their 
administrative burden. HearEase streamlines the hearing scheduling 
process by minimizing conflicts and human errors, providing reliable 
administrative support. By automating complex logistical tasks, officials 
can optimize their time management and focus their energy on 
substantive dispute resolution rather than clerical challenges. This shift 
from manual to digital operations is anticipated to improve workflow 
efficiency and overall job satisfaction.  
 
21 
For the Community and Local Residents  
The system promotes a more accessible and transparent avenue 
for justice. By ensuring hearings are scheduled efficiently and 
communicated clearly, residents are spared the inconvenience of long wait 
times or unannounced cancellations. A more organized justice system 
fosters public trust, encouraging citizens to utilize proper legal channels 
for dispute resolution rather than resorting to informal or escalating 
conflicts.  
For the Local Government Units (LGUs) and DILG  
This study serves as a proof-of-concept for digital transformation at 
the grassroots level. The data generated by HearEase provides LGUs with 
valuable insights into crime trends, case volumes, and resolution rates, 
enabling data-driven decision-making and policy formulation. Furthermore, 
this system demonstrates how technology can enhance transparency and 
accountability in public service delivery.  
For Future Researchers and the Academic Community  
This research contributes to the growing body of knowledge 
regarding GovTech (Government Technology) and the application of Deep 
Neural Networks (DNN) in public administration. It serves as a 
foundational reference for future studies aiming to digitize other barangay 
 
22 
processes. The methodologies and datasets established in this study 
provide a benchmark for researchers attempting to solve similar 
scheduling and resource allocation problems in the Philippines.

The Barangay Justice System, or Katarungang Pambarangay, serves as 
the Philippines’ oldest and most community-centered dispute resolution 
mechanism. Established by Presidential Decree No. 1508 in 1978 and later 
strengthened through Republic Act 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991), it 
institutionalized the practice of conciliating minor disputes at the grassroots level 
before elevating them to higher courts [6]. Designed to ease judicial backlogs 
and promote harmony among neighbors, the system underscores mediation, 
conciliation, and arbitration as primary tools of justice .​ 
Contemporary research regards the BJS as one of the most adaptive 
community-based justice systems in Southeast  Asia. Medina and Agustin 
describe it as a hybrid legal institution, merging traditional consensus-building 
with statutory frameworks that empower the Lupon Tagapamayapa–a council of 
mediators appointed by the barangay captain–to pursue amicable settlements 
[7]. A 2025 policy study from the Philippine Information Agency reported the 
rollout of new Barangay Justice System modules across Mindanao, aiming to 
integrate legal frameworks with cultural practices and strengthen peace and 
governance at the barangay level [8].

CHAPTER III​
METHODOLOGY 
 
Research Design 
This study utilizes a Developmental Research Design, specifically 
focusing on the creation, deployment, and evaluation of HearEase, an 
AI-powered hearing scheduling system. This design is appropriate as the study 
aims to produce a functional software artifact that addresses administrative 
inefficiencies through technological intervention. The system will be deployed as 
a Web-Based Application, ensuring accessibility for barangay officials and 
constituents.  
To analyze the system's efficacy, the research employs a Quantitative 
approach. This involves two phases:  
Model Evaluation: Assessing the DNN's performance using standard error 
metrics (MAE, RMSE, R², POISSON DEVIANCE) and prediction accuracy 
against the historical and synthetic datasets from Barangay Tetuan. 
System Evaluation: Conducting User Acceptance Testing (UAT) via ISO 25010 
standards to measure usability, efficiency, and functionality based on feedback 
from the Lupong Tagapamayapa and barangay staff.  
 
42 
Participants and Sampling 
The study will employ a purposive sampling method, as the participants 
will be selected based on their direct involvement and experience with the 
barangay justice system in the pilot site of Barangay Tetuan. The participants will 
consist of two groups:  
 
1. Barangay Officials and Lupong Tagapamayapa (Expert Users)  
This group comprises the primary administrators of the system, 
including the Punong Barangay, Barangay Secretary, and the appointed 
Lupon members. The study aims for Total Enumeration or the inclusion of 
the entire available workforce responsible for mediation, estimated at 10 to 
15 participants. This comprehensive coverage ensures that the feedback 
represents the collective operational needs of the barangay, validating the 
system’s functional suitability and workflow efficiency.  
 
2. Local Residents (End-Users) 
To evaluate the public-facing portal, 25 residents who have prior 
experience with filing complaints or attending hearings will be selected. 
These participants will be engaged for User Acceptance Testing (UAT) to 
assess the system’s usability, interface clarity, and transparency. This 
sample size is sufficient to identify usability flaws and gauge general user 
satisfaction in a developmental pilot study context. 
 
43 
Data Source 
The research data for the purpose of developing an AI technology system 
using Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) to enhance barangay hearings will be from 
primary data and secondary data. The historical case records will present a vital 
component as they will be used to obtain information on previous barangay 
cases. Case records will include details like types of cases, duration of cases, 
predicted number of hearings, resolutions of cases, and any delays observed in 
case processing. These records will be provided by the records management 
division of Barangay Tetuan, Zamboanga City.  
Demographic data on the parties in the cases will also be collected 
including persons' ages, gender, and other socioeconomic status information. 
This will be done to identify trends in handling cases, along with identifying user 
needs. Demographic data will be collected in coordination with barangay officials 
and may also emerge from municipality census files.  
The study will also acquire qualitative and perception data through the 
following means:  
Surveys issued to members of the Lupong Tagapamayapa and barangay 
workers to further understand current practices, perceived challenges, and 
opportunities for enhanced case management of semi-structured interviews of 
barangay officials to gather deeper understandings of operational workflows and 
trends in case thinking. Then, all data collected will be compiled and 
 
44 
pre-processed, prior for analysis. This means structuring historical case data, 
anonymizing sensitive information, coder survey, and interview responses into 
datasets for analysis.  
Lastly, User Acceptance testing, UAT will occur during the system 
evaluation process. Structured feedback and surveys will be used by users to 
assess usability in terms of the effectiveness of the system and user satisfaction. 
The UAT feedback will be an important input information for system refinement 
and evaluating the system's effectiveness on the case management process.  
Through this empirical approach to data gathering means that the 
development of the system is driven by real-world data, contextual reality of the 
human factors operationalized, and the input feedback from the stakeholders 
engaged.

The core dataset for training the Deep Neural Network (DNN) model is 
derived from the actual case blotters of Barangay Tetuan, Zamboanga City,

lockdown, and post-pandemic operational contexts.  
To understand the nuances of the Katarungang Pambarangay workflow, 
semi-structured interviews will be conducted with the Punong Barangay, 
Barangay Secretary, and Lupon members. These interviews focused on 
 
45 
identifying unwritten rules of scheduling, common causes of delays, and the

For system evaluation, perception data will be gathered through UAT

traditional manual procedures.  
All collected data adheres strictly to the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA

Data Analysis 
The data gathered through the aforementioned techniques will be 
analyzed using a Convergent Parallel Mixed Methods approach, integrating both 
quantitative and qualitative findings to provide a holistic assessment of the 
HearEase system.  
1. Data Pre-processing and Cleaning  
Prior to analysis, the historical case records will undergo rigorous 
pre-processing. This involves Data Cleaning to remove duplicate entries or 
formatting errors, Normalization to scale numerical values, and One-Hot 
 
48 
Encoding to convert categorical variables into machine-readable formats. The 
dataset will be split into training (80%) and validation (20%) sets to prepare for 
the Deep Neural Network (DNN) training.  
2. Quantitative Analysis (AI Model Performance)  
To evaluate the effectiveness of the AI prediction engine, the study will 
utilize standard regression error metrics rather than simple accuracy 
percentages. The following metrics will be calculated: 
●​ Mean Absolute Error (MAE) - To measure the average magnitude of 
errors in predicting the number of hearings and resolution days.  
●​ Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) - To penalize larger prediction errors 
and ensure reliability in complex cases. 
●​ Mean Poisson Deviance: Specifically utilized to evaluate the Hearings 
Head of the neural network. Since the number of mediation sessions is 
count data (non-negative integers), Poisson Deviance provides a more 
accurate measure of the model's "goodness-of-fit" than standard squared 
error metrics. A lower deviance value indicates a superior fit for the 
discrete distribution of barangay hearings. 
●​ Coefficient of Determination: Used to quantify the proportion of variance 
in the targets that is predictable from the input features. This metric 
validates the "explanatory power" of the socio-legal variables (Case Type, 
Relationship, and Severity) on the final resolution timeline. 
 
49 
●​ Descriptive Statistics: Measures such as Mean, Median, and Standard 
Deviation were used to summarize the distribution of the 1,199-record 
dataset, ensuring that the stratified sampling maintained the natural 
"Severity Gradient" and "Temporal Patterns" (e.g., COVID-era delays) of 
the original records. 
3. Qualitative Analysis (Interviews and Observations)  
Qualitative data gathered from semi-structured interviews with the Lupong 
Tagapamayapa and operational observations will be subjected to Thematic 
Analysis. This process involves coding responses to identify recurring patterns 
regarding scheduling bottlenecks, administrative pain points, and user readiness 
for digital adoption. These insights will validate the necessity of specific system 
features. 
4. System Evaluation (User Acceptance Testing)  
Data from the User Acceptance Testing (UAT) surveys will be analyzed 
using Descriptive Statistics. The responses, rated on a Likert Scale, will be 
computed for Weighted Means to determine the system’s performance in terms 
of Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, and Usability, adhering to ISO 
25010 Software Quality Standards.  
5. Comparative Analysis  
 
50 
Finally, the study will perform a comparative analysis between the 
traditional manual method and the HearEase automated system. Key 
performance indicators (KPIs), specifically Scheduling Time (minutes) and 
Conflict Detection Rate, will be compared to statistically quantify the efficiency 
gains provided by the AI system. Why 
Software Development Methodology 
This study employs the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) to 
ensure the efficient design, development, and deployment of HearEase. The 
Agile methodology was selected for its iterative nature, allowing for continuous 
refinement based on user feedback and the dynamic operational requirements of 
the barangay justice system.  
 
By utilizing the Scrum framework within Agile, the development process is 
divided into sprint cycles. This approach enables the researchers to address 
functional issues early, adapt to the specific needs of the Lupong 
Tagapamayapa, and ensure that the final output is a robust, user-centric 
administrative and judicial support system. Continuous integration and testing are 
embedded in the workflow to guarantee system stability and data integrity.  
 
Each sprint cycle consists of the following phases:  
●​ Planning: Defining the backlog, user stories, and specific objectives for 
the sprint based on barangay procedural rules.  
 
51 
●​ Design: creating process flowcharts, entity-relationship diagrams (ERD), 
and high-fidelity wireframes to visualize the system architecture.  
●​ Development: Writing the source code for the frontend interfaces and 
backend logic, including the integration of the AI prediction engine and 
scheduling algorithms.  
●​ Testing: Conducting Unit Testing and Integration Testing to ensure all 
modules function correctly and securely.  
●​ Review: Demonstrating the developed features to Barangay Tetuan 
officials to gather immediate feedback and validation. Deployment: Rolling 
out the approved iteration to the live web server for real-world simulation 
and user acceptance. 
Optimization of Hearing Scheduling 
To optimize the scheduling workflow within HearEase, the system employs 
a Hybrid Scheduling Engine that combines AI-driven predictive analytics with a 
rule-based algorithm. This approach ensures that hearing timelines are not only 
historically accurate but also logistically feasible within the barangay’s resource 
constraints. 
1. Predictive Resource Allocation (AI Role) 
The core optimization begins with the Deep Neural Network (DNN) 
Prediction Engine. Upon case filing, the AI analyzes parameters such as case 
severity, party complexity, and historical resolution patterns to forecast the Total 
 
52 
Number of Hearings required. This effectively estimates the "volume" of the case, 
allowing the system to reserve the necessary number of time slots in advance, 
rather than scheduling strictly on a per-hearing basis. 
2. Conflict Detection and Slot Allocation (Algorithm Role) 
Once the required number of hearings is predicted, the system’s Calendar 
Optimization Algorithm calculates specific dates and times. It utilizes a Conflict 
Detection Mechanism to scan the database for existing appointments, holidays, 
and venue availability. If a projected date falls on a non-working day or a fully 
booked slot, the system automatically shifts the schedule to the next available 
working day, ensuring zero overlap and maximizing venue utilization. 
3. Adaptive Scheduling Intervals (Expedite Feature) 
Recognizing that high-stakes cases require swifter resolution, the system 
includes an Adaptive Interval Feature. While standard cases follow the default 
7-day interval (e.g., Monday-to-Monday) to allow for summons delivery, the 
system allows Administrators to toggle an "Expedite Mode" for urgent cases. This 
overrides the standard interval with a shortened duration (e.g., 2–3 days), 
triggering the algorithm to find the earliest available slots while maintaining 
conflict checks. 
4. Automated Document Synthesis 
 
53 
Finally, optimization extends to administrative output. Upon finalizing the 
schedule, the system automatically generates and populates legal documents, 
including Summons and Notices of Hearing, with the specific dates and times 
derived from the optimization engine. This integration eliminates manual 
encoding errors and significantly reduces the time between scheduling and 
document service.

Development 
 
The development of HearEase adheres to the Agile methodology, 
structured into four distinct technical phases: 
 
1. Frontend Development 
●​ The client-side interface is constructed using a modern technology stack 
comprising Tailwind CSS for utility-first styling and shadcn/ui for 
accessible, high-quality UI components. This combination ensures a 
responsive, professional design that adapts to both desktop monitors and 
mobile devices. Visual communication is enhanced through the integration 
of Lucide Icons, providing intuitive cues for non-technical users. 
FullCalendar, a robust JavaScript scheduling library, is utilized to handle 
the complex logic required for rendering hearing dates and interactive 
calendar views.  
2. Backend Development 
The backend of the system was developed using Django REST 
Framework (DRF), an extension of the Django web framework designed for 
building Web APIs. The primary factor for choosing DRF was its ability to provide 
a robust, scalable, and secure interface between the PostgreSQL database and

the frontend application. This setup eliminates the overhead of a traditional 
monolithic server, providing a faster and more scalable user experience. 
●​ Data Management (CRUD): The backend executes secure Create, Read, 
Update, and Delete operations for case records, enforcing referential 
integrity within the database. 
●​ Hybrid Scheduling Engine: This is the core logical module. It acts as the 
bridge between the AI and the calendar. Once the AI predicts the number 
of hearings, this Django module calculates the specific dates (using the 
7-day or Expedited interval), performs Conflict Detection against existing 
appointments, and finalizes the schedule. 
●​ API Architecture: Custom RESTful API endpoints manage the 
communication between the frontend and the AI model, ensuring 
low-latency data transfer during prediction requests. 
●​ Security & Authentication: Firebase Authentication handles session 
management, supporting secure email/password and OAuth protocols. 
Data security is further reinforced at the database level through Supabase 
Row-Level Security (RLS), ensuring that sensitive case blotters are only 
accessible to authorized barangay officials. Communication security is 
maintained via SSL/TLS encryption across all API requests.

3. AI Model Development 
 
●​ Data Preprocessing: The input layer processes a 30-dimensional feature 
vector. This is derived through a multi-step preprocessing pipeline:  
1.​ Ordinal Encoding: Case Severity is mapped to an integer scale 
(Low: 1, Medium: 2, High: 3).  
2.​ One-Hot Encoding: Categorical variables including Case Type (15 
types), Relationship (6 types), and Lockdown Status (3 types) are 
transformed into 27 binary columns.  
3.​ Z-Score Normalization: Numerical features such as the number of 
complainants and respondents are standardized to a mean of 0 and 
standard deviation of 1 using StandardScaler.  
 
●​ Feature Engineering: To optimize prediction accuracy, the model utilizes 
specific engineered features derived from the "Barangay Logic": 
 
59 
○​ Party Complexity: The count of Complainants and Respondents is 
factored in to predict scheduling delays caused by availability 
conflicts. 
○​ Relational Dynamics: The relationship between parties (e.g., 
Family vs. Stranger) is weighted to account for the emotional 
intensity of disputes. 
○​ Contextual Factors: Variables such as Intoxication or Recidivism 
are included to refine the estimated resolution timeline. 
 
●​ Model Architecture:  
 
The HearEase system utilizes a Multi-Output Multilayer Perceptron 
(MLP) architecture built with TensorFlow 2.x and Keras. Unlike standard 
single-task models, this architecture employs a shared feature extraction 
trunk (comprising 128 and 64-unit Dense layers with ReLU activation) that 
branches into two independent prediction heads.  
 
This 'split-head' design allows the model to learn common 
socio-legal patterns in the shared trunk while specializing the output layers 
for two distinct distributions:  
 
1.​ Hearings Head: Predicts a discrete count (1–6 sessions).  
 
60 
2.​ Resolution Head: Predicts a continuous timeframe (3–172 
days). To prevent overfitting on the 1,199-record dataset, a 
Dropout layer (rate=0.2) is integrated into the shared trunk, 
ensuring the model generalizes well to new cases rather 
than memorizing training data.

Evaluation 
The evaluation phase synthesizes the quantitative and qualitative data 
gathered to assess the practical value and efficacy of the HearEase system. This 
process is designed to validate whether the specific research objectives have 
been met, focusing on technical accuracy, user acceptance, and operational 
impact. 
1. Evaluation of AI Prediction Accuracy 
To verify the system's ability to forecast resolution timelines, dataset will 
be used for testing. The model’s predictions will be compared against the actual 
historical values using statistical error metrics: 
 
68 
●​ Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Utilized to quantify the average deviation in 
the predicted number of days and hearing counts. This provides a clear, 
real-world interpretation of the error (e.g., an MAE of 0.70 hearings 
indicates the prediction is usually within one session of the actual result). 
●​ Mean Poisson Deviance: Specifically employed to evaluate the "Total 
Hearings" prediction head. Since mediation sessions are discrete count 
data (non-negative integers), Poisson Deviance provides a more accurate 
measure of the model's goodness-of-fit than standard regression metrics, 
identifying how well the model captures the frequency distribution of 
barangay cases. 
●​ Coefficient of Determination: Used to assess the model's explanatory 
power. This metric measures the proportion of variance in resolution time 
that is predictable from the input features (Severity, Relationship, and 
Case Type), proving that the chosen variables are statistically significant 
drivers of case longevity. 
Accuracy Rate (Margin-based): To measure the percentage of predictions 
that fall within a practically acceptable margin. For Resolution Time, a margin of 
hearing was applied. High success rates in these margins validate the reliability 
of the "Predictive Resource Allocation" feature for administrative use. 
 
69 
2. Assessment of User Acceptance 
Data from the surveys administered to the Lupong Tagapamayapa and 
residents will be analyzed using Descriptive Statistics. User feedback will be 
quantified based on the ISO 25010 Software Quality Standards, specifically 
measuring: 
●​ Functional Suitability: Does the system perform the scheduling tasks as 
required? 
●​ Usability: Is the interface intuitive for non-technical barangay officials? 
●​ High weighted mean scores in these categories will serve as the indicator 
of the system’s successful design and user-friendliness. 
 
4. Data Triangulation 
To ensure the reliability of the findings, the study will employ Data 
Triangulation. Consistency will be cross-verified between the Quantitative Results 
(System Logs/AI Metrics) and the Qualitative Insights (Interview Feedback). For 
instance, if the AI reports a 50% reduction in scheduling time, this should 
correspond with interview statements from the Barangay Secretary regarding 
reduced workload. This holistic approach ensures that the evaluation captures 
both the technical performance and the lived experience of the stakeholders.

4.1 Analysis of Existing Case Workflows 
To identify requirements for automation, the researchers conducted 
interviews and observations at Barangay Tetuan. 
●​ Workflow Constraints: The existing process relies on a manual logbook 
system. Scheduling is done on an ad-hoc basis, usually set exactly 7 days 
apart without considering case complexity. 
●​ Bottlenecks: Key issues identified include overlapping appointments 
(conflicts), difficulty in tracking case history, and inconsistent estimation of 
how many hearings a case might require. 
●​ Requirement for AI: The findings confirmed that a "one-size-fits-all" 7-day 
schedule does not account for high-severity cases or complex 
relationships, justifying the need for a predictive model.

4.2 Data Collection and Dataset Characteristics 
The researchers collected and processed 1,199 stratified records from the 
Barangay Tetuan case blotters (2018–2025). 
●​ Case Distribution: The dataset reflects 15 distinct case types. As shown 
in the findings, Gossip/Oral Defamation (14.0%) and Neighborhood 
Disturbance (12.7%) are the most common. 
●​ Validation of Socio-Legal Patterns: Analysis of the collected data 
revealed a Severity Gradient: Low-severity cases average 2.75 hearings, 
while High-severity cases average 4.05 hearings. Furthermore, Family 
disputes were found to be the most complex, requiring a mean of 4.06 
hearings. 
●​ Data Integrity: The stratified sampling preserved the sharp drop in case 
volume during 2020–2021, representing the COVID-19 lockdown impact, 
which was critical for training the AI to distinguish between case 
complexity and administrative delays.

shared multi-output learning that ensures logically consistent hearings-and-days 
predictions, and scalability to larger datasets where neural networks are 
expected to close the current 1.37% accuracy gap. Ridge Regression remains 
the strongest choice for any deployment where interpretability and training speed 
are prioritized over continuous learning.

CHAPTER V​
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
 
Summary of Findings 
This study successfully developed and implemented HearEase, an 
AI-driven system utilizing Deep Neural Networks (DNN) to optimize barangay 
hearing scheduling and predict case resolution times in Barangay Tetuan, 
Zamboanga City. The research addressed critical inefficiencies in traditional 
manual scheduling processes that often resulted in delays, scheduling conflicts, 
and administrative burden on barangay officials and the Lupong Tagapamayapa. 
Key findings from the study include: 
System Development and Functionality 
HearEase was successfully designed as a web-based platform with 
role-based access for barangay officials and a transparent case-tracking portal 
for residents. The system automates hearing scheduling based on established 
barangay rules, predicts case resolution times, and provides data-driven insights 
for operational optimization. 
 
108 
 
Predictive Accuracy 
The Deep Neural Network model demonstrated strong predictive 
capabilities in forecasting case resolution times and the number of hearings 
required. Through comparative analysis of multiple neural network architectures, 
the selected DNN model achieved optimal performance in terms of accuracy, 
processing speed, and reliability when trained on historical case data from 
Barangay Tetuan. 
User Acceptance and Satisfaction 
User Acceptance Testing (UAT) revealed positive feedback from both 
barangay officials and the Lupong Tagapamayapa. Participants reported that the 
system was user-friendly, intuitive, and significantly improved their workflow. The 
centralized calendar and notification features were particularly valued for keeping 
all stakeholders informed of upcoming hearings and deadlines. 
Data-Driven Decision Making 
The reporting and analytics features of HearEase enabled barangay 
officials to monitor case resolution timelines, identify procedural bottlenecks, and 
make evidence-based decisions to improve service delivery. Historical data 
analysis revealed patterns in case types, resolution durations, and common 
scheduling challenges. 
 
109 
 
Transparency and Accessibility 
The public portal component of HearEase enhanced transparency by 
allowing residents to file complaints digitally and track their case status in 
real-time. This feature improved citizen engagement and trust in the barangay 
justice system while maintaining compliance with legal requirements for 
in-person document service and hearings. 
Scalability Potential 
The pilot implementation in Barangay Tetuan demonstrated that the 
system architecture and methodology could be adapted and scaled to other 
barangays with similar governance structures and caseloads, contributing to 
broader improvements in local justice administration across the region. 
Conclusions 
Based on the findings of this research, the following conclusions are drawn: 
AI Integration Enhances Barangay Justice Administration 
The successful implementation of HearEase demonstrates that artificial 
intelligence and Deep Neural Networks can be effectively integrated into 
grassroots-level governance systems in the Philippines. The technology 
addresses longstanding inefficiencies in manual processes while respecting 
 
110 
 
established legal frameworks and cultural practices inherent in the Katarungang 
Pambarangay system. 
Predictive Analytics Improves Resource Allocation 
The ability to predict case resolution times and hearing requirements 
enables barangay officials to allocate resources more effectively, plan schedules 
proactively, and manage caseloads with greater precision. This predictive 
capability transforms reactive case management into a proactive, data-informed 
process. 
Technology Adoption Requires User-Centric Design 
The positive user acceptance results underscore the importance of 
designing systems that prioritize usability and align with the actual workflows of 
barangay officials. Success in technology adoption at the local government level 
depends not only on technical sophistication but also on intuitive interfaces and 
adequate training support. 
Digital Transformation Enhances Transparency and Trust 
By providing residents with direct access to case information and status 
updates, HearEase strengthens transparency in the barangay justice system. 
This transparency builds community trust and encourages greater civic 
engagement, contributing to more responsive and accountable local governance. 
 
111 
 
Historical Data is Valuable for System Training 
The quality and comprehensiveness of historical case records directly 
impact the accuracy of AI predictions. Barangays that maintain detailed, 
well-organized records are better positioned to benefit from AI-driven systems, 
highlighting the importance of consistent documentation practices. 
Hybrid Approach Balances Innovation and Tradition 
HearEase successfully demonstrates that technological innovation can 
complement rather than replace traditional dispute resolution practices. The 
system enhances administrative efficiency while preserving the human-centered, 
community-based nature of barangay hearings and the essential role of the 
Lupong Tagapamayapa in mediation and conciliation. 
Pilot Implementation Validates Broader Applicability 
The success of the pilot study in Barangay Tetuan provides strong 
evidence that similar AI-driven scheduling and prediction systems can be 
implemented in other barangays throughout Zamboanga City and potentially 
across the Philippines, contributing to nationwide improvements in local justice 
administration. 
 
112

Hearing Scheduling Process 
A hearing schedule is assigned to 
each case, followed by a summon 
letter personally delivered by the 
Barangay Officials to the respondent’s 
address. Delivering Summons 
in-person is mandatory, despite the 
use of any e-barangay systems. 
Role of Lupon Members 
The Lupon Member who receives the 
complaint is responsible for facilitating 
the hearing on its scheduled date.   
Lupon Duty Schedule 
If Lupon 1 is on duty on Monday, and 
receives a case, Lupon 1 is 
automatically obligated to handle the 
hearing next Monday, and on the next 
 
123 
 
Monday if the hearing is not settled. 
Types of Cases Handled 
Minor disputes (e.g. property conflicts) 
are resolved through barangay 
mediation hearings. 
Cases Immediately Forwarded to 
Police Authority 
Severe offenses (e.g., murder and 
physical violence) are immediately 
escalated to the police or higher 
authorities, and not catered for 
barangay hearings. 
Identified Challenges 
Manual scheduling and tracking often 
leads to delays and inefficiency. 
Suggested Improvements 
The officials expressed interest in 
implementing an automated 
scheduling system to streamline 
processes and reduce manual work.
